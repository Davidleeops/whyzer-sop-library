## SECTION 1: THE SALESFORCE BUYER MAP

### Economic Buyer (VP/SVP/CRO/CFO)
Controls budget and signs off on CapEx decisions. Cares about: pipeline predictability, revenue per rep, cost of sale, time-to-close. Does NOT want to hear about features — only business outcomes that move the P&L.

### Champion (Director/Senior Manager level)
Feels the pain daily. Often a RevOps Director, Sales Enablement Lead, or Sales Director. They need to build the internal business case. Your job: arm them with the financial language their CFO will approve.

### User Buyer (AE, SDR, Sales Manager)
The person who will use CapEx Layer day-to-day. Their buy-in matters for adoption but they rarely control budget. Focus: time saved, quota attainment, ease of use.

### Coach (Internal advocate with access and influence)
Someone who wants you to win. May be in IT, operations, or an aligned VP. Cultivate early. Ask: "Who else needs to be a part of this conversation?"

## SECTION 2: KEY FINANCIAL TRIGGERS FOR SALESFORCE CUSTOMERS

1. Salesforce renewal cycle — Large Salesforce contracts renew annually. Budget is allocated 60-90 days prior. Target companies 3-4 months before renewal.
2. Sales team headcount growth — Hiring surges signal revenue pressure. More reps = more onboarding cost = more urgency for productivity tools.
3. CRO or VP Sales turnover — New leadership creates 90-day window. They are re-evaluating the stack and want quick wins.
4. Missed quota or declining close rates — Visible in public earnings calls, LinkedIn posts from leaders, press coverage. Creates urgency around rep productivity.
5. Salesforce implementation/upgrade — Companies that just went live on Salesforce are actively looking for add-ons that justify the core investment.
6. Series B/C funding events — Growth-stage companies scaling their sales team are investing in productivity infrastructure.
7. Board-level revenue pressure — Public signals in earnings calls or investor materials.
8. High rep turnover — Signals friction in the sales process. CapEx Layer reduces ramp time and makes Salesforce easier to use.

## SECTION 3: THE CAPEX LAYER POV FRAMEWORK (5-STEP)

The POV (Point of View) is NOT a pitch. It is a financial narrative that positions you as a peer, not a vendor.

**Step 1 - Macro Context (Observation)**
State a macro trend that is undeniably true and creating pressure for this buyer's business. Do NOT mention CapEx Layer. Stay in their world.

Example: "Enterprise software budgets are under more scrutiny than they have been in a decade. Every dollar of Salesforce spend is expected to produce measurable pipeline and revenue outcomes."

**Step 2 - Signal (What you observed about THEM)**
Reference a specific, verifiable signal about this company. Earnings call, press release, LinkedIn post, job posting, funding announcement. Green = fact. Yellow = inference. Blue = estimate.

Example: "I noticed [Company] posted 3 Salesforce Admin roles in the last 60 days [Green]. That typically signals either a major implementation push or adoption challenges across the sales team [Yellow]."

**Step 3 - Tension (The gap between where they are and where they need to be)**
Name the financial tension without proposing a solution. The tension IS your punchline setup.

Example: "When Salesforce adoption is inconsistent, the data quality degrades — and when data quality degrades, forecast accuracy drops. That is a direct cost: misallocated quota, wrong territory plans, missed board-level commitments."

**Step 4 - Resolution (New approach / reframe)**
Introduce a category shift, not a product. Show them there is a different way to think about the problem.

Example: "The companies hitting 90%+ Salesforce adoption are not doing it through training. They are embedding productivity directly into the rep's workflow — making the right action the path of least resistance."

**Step 5 - CTA (Low-friction next step)**
Never ask for a demo. Ask for a conversation about their situation.

Example: "I would like to understand whether [Company] is facing this pattern before assuming. Would 20 minutes be worth it this week?"

## SECTION 4: TENSION STATEMENT TEMPLATES FOR SALESFORCE CUSTOMERS

### Template A - Adoption & ROI
"[Company] is investing [X] per seat per year in Salesforce. When adoption falls below [threshold], that investment becomes a liability — not an asset. The financial exposure is not just the license cost; it is the downstream impact on forecast accuracy and quota attainment."

### Template B - Rep Productivity
"The average Salesforce user spends [X] hours per week on data entry and manual workflow management. At [Company]'s current headcount, that is [X] hours of lost selling time per quarter — equivalent to [X] full-time reps. That is a cost the income statement is already carrying."

### Template C - New Leadership Window
"When a new CRO or VP Sales joins, there is typically a 90-day window before the existing stack becomes legacy. During that window, the tools that can show direct pipeline impact earn permanent budget. The tools that cannot get rationalized. This is that window for [Company]."

## SECTION 6: EXECUTIVE OUTREACH - THREE-LEGGED STOOL EMAIL

The Three-Legged Stool principle: Every cold email must have (1) a specific, verifiable observation about the company, (2) a financial tension that is real and material, (3) a low-friction CTA that respects the buyer's time.

### Subject Line Options (test all three)
- A: "Salesforce ROI at [Company]"
- B: "[Observation] - quick question"
- C: "Re: [Company]'s Q[X] revenue target"

### Email Body

Subject: Salesforce ROI at [Company]

[First name],

[Company] just [funded/announced/hired for X] — which usually means Salesforce is under more pressure to show direct revenue contribution. [GREEN SIGNAL]

When adoption is inconsistent, the upstream data quality degrades — and the downstream cost is forecast error and missed pipeline targets. [TENSION]

The companies resolving this fastest are not retraining their reps. They are changing the workflow so the right behaviour is the easiest behaviour. [REFRAME]

Worth 20 minutes to see if this pattern is relevant at [Company]?

[Name]
CapEx Layer

P.S. Happy to show you the adoption model — takes 10 minutes to run and benchmarks against companies your size.

## SECTION 7: THE 5-TOUCH OUTREACH SEQUENCE

Target: 40% reply rate by email #4 (Kishan Patel benchmark)

Touch 1 (Day 1): The POV email (above). Lead with observation + tension. No product mention.

Touch 2 (Day 3): Short follow-up. One new signal. "Saw [Company] also [new observation]. Adds another dimension to the earlier point."

Touch 3 (Day 7): Social proof without naming the client. "A RevOps team at a comparable Salesforce customer resolved this in one quarter. Happy to share how they framed the business case internally."

Touch 4 (Day 14): Direct ask + breakup framing. "I have sent a few notes. If Salesforce adoption is not a priority right now, I understand — just let me know and I will stop reaching out. If it is, I would love 20 minutes."

Touch 5 (Day 21): Final value add. Share a resource, insight, or framework. Leave on a high note. "Regardless of timing, this framework for calculating Salesforce ROI might be useful for your next planning cycle."

## SECTION 8: BUSINESS CASE STRUCTURE & APPROVAL THRESHOLDS

### Understanding Approval Thresholds
- Under $25K: Director-level approval common. Shorter cycle (2-4 weeks).
- $25K-$100K: VP-level involvement required. Business case needed. 4-8 weeks.
- Over $100K: C-suite or board approval. Full ROI model required. 8-16 weeks.

### Business Case One-Pager Structure
1. Current State: What is the problem costing today? (Quantify in dollars)
2. Root Cause: Why is it happening? (Adoption gap, workflow friction, data quality)
3. Proposed Solution: CapEx Layer — what it does and how it integrates with Salesforce
4. Financial Impact: Expected improvement in adoption rate / rep productivity / ramp time — translated to revenue impact
5. Investment Required: Annual cost per seat, total contract value
6. Payback Period: Months to break even based on conservative improvement assumptions
7. Risk of Inaction: What does it cost to do nothing? (Quantify)
8. Recommended Next Step: Pilot structure, timeline, success metrics

### Pre-Answering the CFO's Questions
- "How do we know this will stick?" — Pilot data from comparable companies + 90-day adoption guarantee
- "What is the integration complexity?" — Native Salesforce integration, no IT lift required
- "What if our reps do not use it?" — Change management support included, adoption metrics tracked
- "Can we buy this later?" — Current pricing locked. Headcount expansion adds cost. Now is optimal entry.
- "What is the downside risk?" — Defined pilot exit clause. No long-term obligation until pilot KPIs met.

## SECTION 9: QUICK-REFERENCE CHECKLISTS

### Pre-Outreach Checklist
- Identified the Economic Buyer (name, title, LinkedIn verified)
- Found 1-2 Green signals about the company (verifiable facts)
- Identified 1 Yellow signal (reasonable inference from facts)
- Translated operational observation to financial tension
- Confirmed company is on Salesforce (tech stack tool or job posting language)
- Checked for recent trigger event (funding, leadership change, earnings, hiring)
- Subject line tested (use 3 variants across similar accounts simultaneously)
- CTA is low-friction (20 minutes, not "demo request")

### POV Quality Checklist
- Does the email mention CapEx Layer by name? (If yes — REMOVE IT from first touch)
- Is the observation specific and verifiable? (General statements = delete)
- Is the tension stated in financial language? (Features = fail)
- Does the CTA ask for a conversation, not a demo?
- Is the email under 120 words? (Shorter = higher reply rate)

### Deal Health Checklist (weekly review)
- Economic Buyer engaged (not just champion)?
- Financial tension confirmed in their language (not yours)?
- Business case in progress (not just a demo scheduled)?
- Approval threshold understood (know the decision-making process)?
- Timeline agreed (not assumed)?
- Risk of inaction established (they know the cost of waiting)?

## SECTION 10: KEY MINDSET PRINCIPLES & MASTER QUOTES

1. The product is the punchline. The problem is the headline. Never lead with CapEx Layer. Lead with their financial reality.

2. Stay in business language until THEY ask about the product. The moment you describe features before they ask, you have become a vendor. Stay a peer.

3. You are not selling software. You are selling a financial outcome. CapEx Layer is the mechanism. The outcome is Salesforce ROI, rep productivity, pipeline predictability.

4. Green / Yellow / Blue credibility labelling. Green = verified fact. Yellow = reasonable inference. Blue = estimate. Never present inferences as facts.

5. Every claim needs a number. "Increases productivity" is meaningless. "Reduces admin time by 4 hours per rep per week, equivalent to $X in recovered selling capacity" is a business case.

6. The champion needs to be able to sell this internally without you in the room. Build that capability in them.
